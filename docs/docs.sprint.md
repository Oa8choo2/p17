# docs.sprint

A documentation sprint (or “doc sprint”) is a focused, time-boxed event—usually
lasting from a few days to a week—where a team or community gathers to write,
update, or reorganize project documentation.
[1]
(https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2019/07/22/how-to-run-an-open-source-doc-sprint/),
[2] (https://www.docsie.io/blog/glossary/sprint/)

Key Steps to Run a Documentation Sprint

• Define the Scope: Pick a clear, achievable goal (e.g., updating the API
reference, writing onboarding guides, or clearing a backlog of help articles).
[1] (https://www.docsie.io/blog/glossary/sprint/), [2]
(https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2019/07/22/how-to-run-an-open-source-doc-sprint/)
• Prepare the Infrastructure: Set up your writing platform (such as GitHub,
Notion, or Confluence), style guides, and pre-formatted templates before day
one. [1] (https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2012/09/07/how-to-plan-a-doc-sprint/),
[2] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Q7905qBUyQ&t=66), [3]
(https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2019/07/22/how-to-run-an-open-source-doc-sprint/)
• Onboard and Train: Host short 10-minute training sessions on your tools,
markdown/markup rules, or content style so everyone starts on the same page.
[1] (https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2012/09/07/how-to-plan-a-doc-sprint/) •
Organize into Pairs or Pods: Pair subject-matter experts (engineers or product
leads) with technical writers or general contributors.
[1] (https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2012/09/07/how-to-plan-a-doc-sprint/) •
Track Progress on a Board: Use a Kanban or sprint board to move documentation
tasks from To Do to In Progress and Review.
[1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jiTI2DJprWg&t=2), [2]
(https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnuKNiWiY30&t=36) • Host a Demo/Wrap-up:
Dedicate the final hours to reviewing what was written, celebrating completions,
and linking finished docs.

(https://ffeathers.wordpress.com/2019/07/22/how-to-run-an-open-source-doc-sprint/)
